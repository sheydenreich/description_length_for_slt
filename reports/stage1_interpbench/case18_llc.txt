Reading. (1) The estimate is not stable in the hyper-parameters: it grows with the chain length (1.0 ->
12.7 at resid_2, 6.4 -> 18.2 at resid_1) and with the step size (x10 step: x28 and x10), and single chains
run away (49.6, 66.9, 103). On the oracle the same settings (step 1e-4, gamma 10, 1000 steps) read the
effective coefficient within 20-30% (reports/oracle/llc_vs_regret_slope.txt). (2) The chain is drifting,
not sampling: the loss keeps rising along the flat directions of the saturated readout, which the
localisation (gamma 10 in scale units) does not confine in 1000-2000 steps; weaker localisation (gamma 1, a weaker pull to the MAP than gamma 10)
gives the only stable reading, 2.2 +- 0.2, but then the chains still drift and the number is set by the pull as much as by the
landscape. (3) What is reproduced: the ordering (the misspecified locus resid_1 is higher in every
setting, as its regret slope is 13x higher) and the order of magnitude at the valid locus (2 lambda of
about 4 directions against 5-7 from the Fisher spectrum and 16 from the regret slope 8 +- 3). What is not
reproduced is the number, so the LLC is not used as a summary column. The regret slope of the MAP code
remains the estimator of the effective coefficient; this table is a diagnostic. Loss traces per chain were
added to the estimator (LLCEstimate.traces) to separate drift from plateau; see case18_llc_traces.txt.

Per-chain traces (case18_llc_traces.txt, 2000 steps, integrand n*beta*(L - L*) every 200 steps). No chain
reaches a plateau in any setting. Valid locus, step 1e-4, pull 10: three chains sit at 0.0-0.8 for all
2000 steps while the fourth leaves the flat region after ~600 steps and grows without bound (4.5, 9.0,
16.7, 41.1, 56.6, 85.0 at steps 800-1800). Valid locus, pull 1: all four chains grow steadily from 0 to
7-21. Step 1e-5: nothing moves (integrand 0.00 throughout). Misspecified locus, step 1e-4: all chains
grow steadily (pull 10: 1 -> 17-32; pull 1: 2 -> 26-63). Reading: the loss is exactly flat around the MAP
over the localisation scale (saturated readout: moving the span does not change any prediction until a
target crosses the decision boundary), so the tempered posterior at beta = 1/ln n is not a local object
at this n; a chain either sees nothing or escapes the flat region and then its loss grows like the
distance travelled. The sampler is therefore measuring the size and the boundary of the flat region, not
a power law at a point, which is why its number depends on step, chain length and pull. The regret slope
at larger n, where boundary targets appear in the data, is the quantity that resolves this geometry.

Sampler on a MAP fitted to 8192 targets (case18_llc_traces_n8192.txt; here the regret slope is 17-21
over the whole range and ~45 over the last doublings). Same pathology, stronger: with n*beta about 910
instead of 148 the drift term is six times larger. Step 1e-4, pull 10: two chains sit near 0 for 1000
steps, two escape at ~400-600 steps and grow to 160-220 by step 1800; reading 88 +- 36. Step 1e-5:
0.01 (nothing moves). Pull 100: chains jump between 0 and 1200 from one evaluation to the next (the pull
term overshoots at this step size); reading 173 +- 50. No setting produces a plateau near the regret
slope. The estimator's number is not the coefficient of the code at any n tried.
