Reading (2026-10-20). At the MAP of the d_V = 1 subspace map on the oracle (d = 12, n = 2048, prior
precision 1), the tangent-space Hessian of the mean loss has 11 curved directions for every k: the k + 1
directions inside the data's subspace (eigenvalues ~0.8-1.1, with the V-noise plane at k = 1 giving
0.89 and 0.064) and the 11 - k directions outside it, which are curved (h = 9e-5 ... 0.03, growing
with k) but carry NO gradient variance: the Fisher (covariance of per-example gradients) is 1e-16 there
against F/h ~ 1 inside. Every example pushes the frame out of those directions identically, so the
empirical minimiser sits at zero in them without sampling noise and the plug-in code pays nothing:
(1/2) tr(F H^+) = 1.1, 1.1, 2.4, 3.3, 4.5 for k = 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 against the measured MAP-code slopes
0.53, 1.46, 1.94, 2.24, 4.49 (the k = 1 value is low because the weakly curved second direction,
h = 0.064, is not resolved by 100 warm-started L-BFGS steps per block). The Bayes code pays for every
curved direction once n resolves it: the Laplace prediction (1/2) sum n h / (n h + 1), averaged over
n = 256..2048, is 1.3, 4.3, 3.3, 5.4, 5.5 against the measured HMC slopes 2.0, 2.9, 3.2, 3.2, 4.7, the
same ordering and magnitude (the Laplace numbers are rough: the prior on the raw frame is not the
uniform prior on the sphere that the scale-free map actually has, and the outside curvature at k = 2
and 4 sits right at the resolution threshold n h ~ 1). Conclusion: on this family the information
equality F = H fails in the deterministically constrained directions, so the plug-in's prequential
coefficient (1/2) tr(F H^-1) is below the Bayes learning coefficient, which counts curved directions
of H regardless of F. The earlier statement "lambda_eff = k/2" describes the plug-in (MAP) code, not
the Bayes code.

Correction (2026-10-22, review item 1.8). The 11 - k outside directions are not curved: in the population
they are quartic (tilting the frame by phi towards them moves the prediction at order phi^2, the KL at
order phi^4; reports/oracle/bayes_code_hmc.txt, end). Their small Hessian eigenvalues here, identical across
the outside directions and growing with k (9e-5 at k = 1 to 0.03 at k = 7), are the cross term of
K = a |phi_r|^2 + c (|phi_r|^2 + |phi_e|^2)^2 evaluated at a MAP whose readable components carry sampling
error: d^2K/dphi_e^2 = 4 c |phi_r|^2, which vanishes as n grows. A Laplace count built on them therefore
mis-models these directions, and the "pred Bayes slope" column is superseded. The Bayes coefficient is
lambda = k/2 + (11 - k)/4 (1/2 per readable, 1/4 per quartic direction), not (d - 1)/2, and the measured
HMC slopes at 16384 targets agree with it (3.35, 3.05, 3.46, 4.44, 5.43). The F/H analysis of the readable
directions and the plug-in prediction (1/2) tr(F H^+) stand.
