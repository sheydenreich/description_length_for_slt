Reading. (1) Here the Bayes code is the shorter one: 230 bits shorter than the plug-in for the valid
alignment, because the posterior predictive hedges where the single map overfits the early blocks, and
the complexity it must pay (regret 88 nats at 1024, slope 13, a quarter of the nominal 56) is small
against the 1000-target loss gain. (2) The Bayes code charges the coupling family what the plug-in does
not: the same family that gains 586 bits as a plug-in loses 322 bits as a Bayes code, because the prior
mass of the faithful coupling maps is tiny (the posterior has not concentrated after 1024 targets and
the predictive stays near the prior, IIA 0.5). The valid rotation alignment therefore beats the coupling
by 1055 bits under the Bayes code, whereas under the plug-in the coupling is 81 bits shorter: the Bayes code's ordering of
families is by learning coefficient, as the plan's T4 intends. (3) The misspecified locus h2 moves from
-405 (plug-in) to +81 (Bayes): the hedged predictive is better than label-only on the 68% of targets it
gets right and does not pay the plug-in's overconfident losses. (4) Together with InterpBench (width
260, nominal 512: Bayes code longer than label-only at 1024 targets, case18_bayes_code_hmc.txt) and
GPT-2 (width 768, nominal 6016: a Bayes code would cost ~65,000 nats, curvature_gpt2.txt), the three
testbeds give the ladder in n versus lambda: the Bayes code is the right instrument when lambda ln n is
small against the loss gain (toy), becomes uncompetitive at a few hundred (InterpBench at 1024, usable
at 8192) and unusable at thousands; the plug-in code's coefficient (1/2) tr(F H^-1) stays at tens
everywhere, which is why the plug-in is the operative Stage 1 code.
