MIB causal-variable track, two-digit addition, variable "carry" (Mueller et al. 2025; mib-bench/
arithmetic_addition, template "Q: How much is N plus N? A: "; bitsev.testbeds.mib). Stage 1 protocol as
for IOI (1024 targets, noise 0.05, r = 2, d_V 16, single-map MAP learner, 400 map steps; manufactured alignment =
coupling, 4000 steps, untrained twin). Output alphabet = distinct first tokens of "0".."198": whole
numbers for Llama (199 symbols), the first digit for Gemma (10 symbols), hence the different label-only
codes. Counterfactual: answer with the source's carry and the base's digit sums.
Summaries: arith_llama31_8b_summary.md (job 1816980, H100, 16.5 h), arith_gemma2_2b_summary.md (job 1817691,
A100, 9.5 h). Gains over label-only in bits (IIA i.i.d.).

The trivial floor. Prompts share their last token, so at layer 0 the read-position state is the same for
every prompt, the interchange does nothing and the predictor is the model's own answer on the base. It is
right whenever base and source have the same carry: about half the pairs for Llama's whole-number readout
(IIA 0.48) and 0.78 of the pairs for Gemma's first-digit readout (the first digit survives a carry change
more often). This floor codes the targets far below label-only (Llama +2715, Gemma +719) and is exactly
what the wrong-locus control measures here; every alignment has to be judged against it, not against
label-only. (The "attainable IIA" printed at setup, 0.47-0.54 on Llama, is this floor and matches the
52% reported for DAS on this task in MIB.)

Reading. (1) On Gemma the carry is linearly intervenable at one locus: layer 20 of 26 lifts the IIA
from the 0.78 floor to 0.96 and shortens the code by 754 bits against the floor; the layers before and
after fall back to the floor (0.78-0.84). The code locates the variable by a margin the controls cannot
match. (2) On Llama the 16-dimensional subspace never leaves the floor (IIA 0.44-0.51 at every layer), as
in the MIB benchmark where linear methods score at chance on this task, and the extra 650-1050 bits it
gains over the floor come from a better-calibrated predictor, not from reading the carry. The coupling
family on the trained network does read it: IIA 0.94-0.96 at layers 20-24 and, at layer 24, the
shortest code of the run (+5063, 1300 bits below the subspace). Its code at layer 20 is about 3200 bits worse
than at 24 despite the same IIA: the nonlinear learner needs most of the 1024 targets to find the map
there, and the prequential code charges for it. On the untrained twin the same family reaches IIA 0.14
and loses 2160 bits to label-only, so the layer-24 coupling alignment is not an artefact of the family's
expressiveness. (3) Across the two models the code ranks the alignments the opposite way (subspace on
Gemma, coupling on Llama) and in each case the winner is the one that is faithful at that locus; a
single family or a single IIA threshold would have missed one of the two. The manufactured alignments
reach IIA 0.14 (Llama) and 0.62 (Gemma) and never beat label-only.
