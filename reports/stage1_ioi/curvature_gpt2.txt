Hessian and Fisher of the learned subspace's loss at its MAP, GPT-2 IOI layer 11 (d_V 16, 12288 frame
parameters, 1024 targets, prior precision 0.01; job 1813999, 2026-10-20; curvature_gpt2_layer11_full.json).
Dense Hessian of the mean loss from 12288 Hessian-vector products (math attention backend), exact
eigendecomposition; empirical Fisher = covariance of the 1024 per-example gradients at the MAP (mean
gradient norm 1e-2: the MAP of 100 L-BFGS steps is not exactly stationary).

Spectrum: 12032 positive eigenvalues and 256 at zero (slightly negative numerically) = d_V^2, the gauge
directions W -> W A that leave the span unchanged. The positive part is nearly flat: top 1.04, median
0.051, minimum 2e-5; 12014 directions above 1% of the top. Every direction that moves the span costs
loss at a comparable rate: at this point the subspace family is a regular model on the Grassmannian,
with the nominal coefficient d_V (d - d_V) / 2 = 6016.
Fisher: trace 15.0 against the Hessian's 683 (2.2%); in the Hessian's eigendirections F_ii / h_i has
median 0.005, mean 0.012, 10-90% range 0.003-0.011 (0.3-0.6 only in the top dozen directions).

Predicted coefficients: Laplace Bayes (1/2) sum n h / (n h + alpha) = 6016 at every n >= 1024 (all
directions resolved); plug-in (1/2) tr(F H^-1) = 71, of which 14 from the top 100 directions and 24
from the top 1000. Measured MAP-code coefficient: 49 +- 2 (n <= 1024), 45 (8192), 37 / 31 / 19
(32768, three fit ranges).

Reading. (1) The mechanism found on the oracle (hessian_vs_fisher.txt) holds on the language model, a
hundredfold stronger: the information equality F = H fails in almost every direction, F being about 1%
of H. Moving the span in a typical direction changes the loss of every target in the same way (a
deterministic cost), so the empirical minimiser has almost no sampling variance there and the plug-in
code pays nothing for those directions; it pays (1/2) tr(F H^-1) ~ 70, within a factor 1.5 of the
measured 45-50 (the formula assumes the exact minimiser and the training-set Fisher; the generalisation
Fisher on fresh targets is computed next). (2) A Bayes code of this family would pay the full 6016
ln n: at n = 1024 about 65,000 nats by the Laplace count. [Corrected 2026-10-22: no code of the observed
law can pay that. Every predictor mixes in the channel, so a target costs at most -ln(eps/K) = 6.46 nats
(eps 0.05, K 32): at most 6565 nats on 1016 coded targets, 2.2x the label-only code's 2998 nats. The Laplace
count exceeds this ceiling until n ~ 10^4, i.e. the asymptotic expansion does not apply at n = 1024; the
statement is that a Bayes code of this family has barely left its prior there, bounded by the ceiling.] The prior mass near the right
16-plane in a 12032-dimensional Grassmannian is astronomically small, and the Bayes code must pay for
it; the plug-in finds the plane by gradient descent along directions that carry no noise. This is why
the Laplace and Langevin "Bayes codes" of October 17-18 collapsed onto the prior and why a Bayes
complexity charge never appeared: for alignment families on real networks the Bayes learning
coefficient is the wrong quantity, and the plug-in's kappa = (1/2) tr(F H^-1) is the operative
complexity. (3) The ordering of explanations under the plug-in code is therefore an ordering by
Fisher-weighted curvature, not by nominal dimension: the coupling map (1.9 M parameters) has kappa
~170-225 and the manufactured alignment ~650-990 because their Fisher is large in many more directions
(every new base/source pair moves their fit). Figure: reports/talk_slt/gpt2_hessian_vs_fisher.png.

Reading. (1) The plug-in prediction is within 1.1x of the measurement at d_V 4 (75 vs 79) and within
1.45x at d_V 16 (71 vs 49), the same accuracy as on the oracle. (2) The measured coefficient of the valid
subspace barely moves with the dimension of the family (79, 49, 56 at d_V 4, 16, 64) while the nominal
Bayes coefficient grows fifteen-fold (1528 to 22528): the plug-in pays for the Fisher-weighted
directions, whose number is set by the data (how many name directions carry sampling noise), not by
the parameter count. The learner's code of the valid alignment gains +132 bits at d_V 4 (IIA 0.27: a
4-plane cannot carry 16 names), +3385 at d_V 16 and +3499 at d_V 64; the family's size is paid for
by the loss term at d_V 4 and by almost nothing at d_V 64. (3) At layer 12 (the post-norm state) the
loss is sharper (top eigenvalue 24 against 1) and 960 directions are numerically negative: the final
norm makes the map's loss non-convex along the radial directions, and the predicted coefficient
(112) is the largest; the measurement at that locus is pending. (4) The control coefficients scale the
other way: the manufactured alignment's coefficient is 817 / 988 / 652 and the trained coupling's
389 / 226 / 160 at d_V 4 / 16 / 64, 3-5x (trained coupling) and 10-20x (manufactured) above the valid subspace at every d_V.
