Which term carries the gain of the valid layer-11 subspace (d_V 16) over each control?

  G(n) = DL_C(n) − DL_H(n) = [Fit_C(n) − Fit_H(n)] + [Reg_C(n) − Reg_H(n)]
  Fit_X(n): loss of X's final fit (on all 32768 targets) on the first n targets   -> ≈ n·Δℓ
  Reg_X(n): prequential code minus Fit_X(n), as in bitsev-h-a3                    -> Δκ ln n + O(1)

Reading. (1) Against label-only, the twin and the wrong locus the gain is pure falsification: the fit term
is the whole gain, the regret term a few hundred bits against the hypothesis. (2) Against the manufactured
alignment the fit term is negative: with enough pairs the flow on the untrained network codes the
counterfactual targets better than the faithful subspace, in-sample, on held-out pairs and in the last
prequential block alike. The whole 7.7k-bit margin is the regret (learning cost) term: the verdict is
Occam's, not falsification's, already at n = 1024. (3) The fit advantage of the flows is calibration, not
causal accuracy: GPT-2 puts ≈ 96 % on the IO name, the subspace swap inherits that confidence, while the
expressive maps push the readout to saturation and match the algorithm's deterministic targets behind the
channel. (4) Consequence: the fit term grows linearly against the hypothesis while the regret term grows
like ln n, so the ordering flips at large n: against the trained coupling near n ≈ 20k (−371 bits at
32768), against the manufactured alignment by rough extrapolation near n ≈ 2·10^5 (regret ≈ 1400 bits
per ln n over 2048-32768, fit −0.06 bits per target). The statements "the manufactured alignment is longer
than label-only" (−1986 bits) and "the coupling codes ~1000 bits behind the subspace" hold at n = 1024; at
32768 the manufactured alignment beats label-only by 108956 bits (summary.md of the run).
(5) Untested remedy: a one-parameter temperature (or a calibrated channel) for every predictor would remove
the calibration advantage and leave the comparison to the regret term.
