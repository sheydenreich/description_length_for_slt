Reading.
(1) The fixed budget was not the cause of the late behaviour: 10 passes per block reproduce every curve within
the seed errors.
(2) Readout as is, the calibration deficit of the valid subspace (≈ 0.05 nats per target) is a falsification
term that grows linearly: against the manufactured alignment the gain peaks near 32768-65536 (≈ 8.3k bits) and
falls to 5.9-6.8k by 131072 (crossing zero by extrapolation at a few 10^5); the trained coupling wins by
5.6-6.9k bits.
(3) Tempered, the margin against the manufactured alignment plateaus at 9-10k bits from 32768 on, no crossover
by 131072; against the trained coupling it is a tie within noise. The plateau means the regret difference no
longer grows like Δκ ln n here (≈ 1100 bits per doubling expected from κ 826 vs 42, 784 nats per ln n times the
batching factor 1.44 times ln 2): the valid subspace's
tempered block rate wobbles between 0.010 and 0.024 above the floor and its held-out final fits sit at
0.057-0.072, i.e. constant-rate Adam leaves an excess loss of order 0.01 nats per target, ≈ 900 bits per 65k
block, comparable to the κ growth. Beyond ~32768 targets this learner's optimisation noise, not its learning
coefficient, sets the per-target excess loss.
(4) The coupling pair keeps separating: +12.8k (as is) and +9.9k (tempered) at 131072. At large n part of it is
fit: the manufactured alignment's block rate stays above the trained coupling's (0.033 vs 0.009 as is).
(5) The fit/regret split is not reliable at 131072 even with 10 epochs: in several seeds the final fit codes the
last block worse in-sample than the block predictor did out of sample (e.g. manufactured, seed 1: 0.082 vs
0.033). A converged final fit needs a decaying learning rate or an L-BFGS polish, not more steps.
For the talk: quote the split at 32768 (3 seeds, slides 14-15) and these gains as the large-n caveat.
