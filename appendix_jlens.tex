\subsection{The Jacobian lens as a parameter-free alignment}
\label{app:topo-jlens}

\LAssess{} The Jacobian lens of \cite{gurnee2026workspace} reads a
mid-layer state through the network's own averaged Jacobian. It was built to
study verbalizable content, but its coordinate swap is a distributed
interchange intervention whose subspace is given analytically rather than
learned. That makes it an alignment family with no fitted parameters beyond
a layer band and a sparsity level, and therefore the floor of the complexity
ladder in the decision experiment, below the identity map only in that it
uses a basis supplied by the network's Jacobian instead of its neurons. The
construction is tied to vocabulary tokens, so it applies to variables whose
values are tokens, such as names or days, and to other variables only
through a token proxy. Its originators call it an imperfect tool, and
commentators have noted an omitted logit-lens comparison, a low
signal-to-noise ratio and the misleading name: the ``J-space'' is a union
of cones, not a subspace.

\paragraph{Construction.} For layer $\ell$, $J_\ell=\E\bigl[\partial
h_{L,t'}/\partial h_{\ell,t}\bigr]$ is the Jacobian of the final-layer
state at later positions $t'\ge t$ with respect to the layer-$\ell$ state,
averaged over positions and about a thousand prompts. The J-vector of token
$w$ is the row $v_w=(W_UJ_\ell)_w$, the average direction at layer $\ell$
that makes $w$ more likely later. The J-space at sparsity $k$ is the set of
nonnegative combinations of at most $k$ J-vectors, fitted by gradient
pursuit; its component carries about 6--15\% of activation variance, so most
of the geometry studied in Appendices~\ref{app:topo-stretch}
and~\ref{app:topo-real} lives in its complement. For a variable $V$ with
token values $w_1,\dots,w_m$, let $V_J=[v_{w_1},\dots,v_{w_m}]$ with columns
normalized to unit length and $Q$ an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal
complement of its span. The J-lens alignment is the linear bijection
\begin{equation}
\phi_J(h)=\bigl(V_J^{+}h,\ Q^{\!\top}h\bigr),\qquad
\phi_J^{-1}(c,u)=V_Jc+Qu,
\label{eq:topo-jlens}
\end{equation}
whose interchange intervention \eqref{eq:topo-interv} transplants the
J-coordinates of the source into the base and leaves the complement of the
base untouched. The in-place swap of \cite{gurnee2026workspace} is the
special case that exchanges two coordinates of one state.

\begin{keybox}{Distortion of the J-lens alignment (internal calculation).}
Let $\sigma_{\max}\ge\sigma_{\min}>0$ be the extreme singular values of
$V_J$. Then
\begin{equation}
\Lip(\phi_J)=\max\Bigl(\frac1{\sigma_{\min}},1\Bigr),\qquad
\Lip(\phi_J^{-1})=\max(\sigma_{\max},1),\qquad
\Dist(\phi_J)=\frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}}=\operatorname{cond}(V_J),
\label{eq:topo-jlens-dist}
\end{equation}
the last equality because unit columns give $\sigma_{\max}\ge1\ge\sigma_{\min}$.
For two values whose J-vectors meet at angle $\theta\le\pi/2$,
$\Dist(\phi_J)=\cot(\theta/2)$. The in-place swap of two J-vectors of equal
norm is a reflection of their plane and hence an isometry.
\end{keybox}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} For $h=h_\parallel+h_\perp$ split along
$\operatorname{span}V_J$ and its complement,
$\|\phi_J(h)\|^2=\|V_J^+h_\parallel\|^2+\|h_\perp\|^2$ and
$\|\phi_J^{-1}(c,u)\|^2=\|V_Jc\|^2+\|u\|^2$, since $V_Jc\perp Qu$; the
suprema are attained on the top and bottom singular directions. Two unit
columns at angle $\theta$ have $\sigma^2=1\pm\cos\theta$. The swap fixes
$v_s+v_t$ and negates $v_s-v_t$, which are orthogonal when the norms are
equal. $\square$

\LNum{} The three identities hold exactly on 30 random bases, 12 angles
and 24 swap configurations (Appendix~\ref{app:topo-checks}).

\paragraph{Consequences for the certificates.} \LCalc{} Because
$\Dist(\phi_J)$ is a property of the network alone, the stretch budget
\eqref{eq:topo-sep} becomes a statement about the network:
$\operatorname{cond}(V_J)\,\Lambda\ge\gamma/\sep_V$. If the J-vectors of the
values of $V$ are well separated, the alignment has no stretch to offer and
must succeed or fail on the network's own separation and sensitivity. The
map is also the mean-field version of the sensitivity term: $W_UJ_\ell$ is
the average of $Dg$ over natural states, so the segment sensitivity
$\Lambda_{b,s,s'}$ of \eqref{eq:topo-budget} splits into the part predicted
by the averaged Jacobian and a residual. Writing $\Delta=h'(b,s)-h'(b,s')$
and $\delta$ for the actual logit difference, the \emph{Jacobian
consistency}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{JC}_{b,s,s'}=\frac{\|\delta-W_UJ_\ell\Delta\|}{\|\delta\|}
\label{eq:topo-jc}
\end{equation}
is small when the intervention acts through the directions the downstream
computation uses on average, and large when it relies on local sensitivity
that the average does not show. The latter is what a manufactured alignment
paying its budget through off-manifold sensitivity must do. Two cautions
remain. A J-vector is a direction that \emph{would} change the output, not
one the model demonstrably uses; this is the setting of the interpretability
illusion of \cite{makelov2024}, so the realizability statistics D3 are the
right complement. And the J-lens cannot route information across positions;
on a task that requires it, a low score reflects the locus, not the
variable.

\begin{hypbox}{H-A10 (the J-lens rival).}
On token-valued variables in the decision experiment, the J-lens alignment
reaches an interchange-intervention accuracy within the margin of the best
rotation at loci where the value is verbalizable, at zero parametric cost,
and $\operatorname{cond}(V_J)$ stays below a small constant. On untrained
networks it fails for copied variables such as the indirect-object name,
where learned reversible maps succeed, because it has no freedom to route.
Manufactured alignments that pass held-out IIA show Jacobian consistency
\eqref{eq:topo-jc} well above that of valid ones on the same pairs.
\end{hypbox}

\paragraph{Version 7.3 (19 October 2026): the Jacobian lens as a rival.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Appendix~\ref{app:topo-jlens} adds the Jacobian lens of
\cite{gurnee2026workspace} as a parameter-free linear alignment for
token-valued variables. Its distortion is the condition number of the
matrix of J-vectors, a property of the network alone, so the stretch budget
becomes a network statement.
\item The averaged Jacobian splits the sensitivity term of the budget into a
predicted part and a residual; the Jacobian-consistency statistic joins
diagnostic D2. Hypothesis H-A10 and proof target T7f are added.
\end{enumerate}
